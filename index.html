I develop statistical theory and computational methods for learning from complex, high-dimensional data. My research connects Bayesian inference, robust statistics, optimal transport, and modern machine learning—with an emphasis on methods that remain reliable when models and data are imperfect.

Current research directions

Reliable inference for
complex data.

My group works at the intersection of statistical foundations, scalable computation, and applications where uncertainty and model misspecification matter.

01 / THEORY

Bayesian foundations

Posterior concentration, Bayesian nonparametrics, high-dimensional models, and theoretical guarantees for approximate inference.

02 / ROBUSTNESS

Robust statistical inference

Methods that account for misspecification and distribution shift, including transport-based formulations and distributional reweighting.

03 / COMPUTATION

Statistical machine learning

Efficient Bayesian computation, variational methods, simulation-based inference, and learning algorithms for structured data.

Background & overview.

Building principled statistical methods requires both mathematical insight and a close connection to the scientific questions that motivate the data.

Background

I received my Ph.D. in Statistical Science from Duke University and my M.Stat. and B.Stat. from the Indian Statistical Institute, Kolkata. My previous faculty appointments include the University of Wisconsin–Madison, Texas A&M University, and Florida State University.
